\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning on benign data measurably degrades refusal in aligned language models, and standard visual instruction tuning erodes the safety of vision-language models; whether an \emph{action-level} safeguard in a vision-language-action (VLA) policy survives benign personalization has not been tested. We install a refusal behavior in OpenVLA-7B by supervised LoRA training on paired scene states (harmful instruction $\to$ hold still; benign instruction $\to$ move), then personalize the policy on $N\in\{50,200\}$ unrelated benign demonstrations and re-measure refusal, over-refusal, old- and new-task success, and closed-loop contact with a static human hand, all on identical initial states, against a comparator trained without the safeguard. \todo{We find that \ldots\ refusal \ldots\ while contact \ldots\ and old-task success \ldots.} This is a single-seed pilot of a protocol; we release the paired-state evaluator, the patched contact predicate, and all instruction templates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Safety behaviors installed by fine-tuning are known to be fragile under further fine-tuning. Qi et al.~\cite{qi2023finetuning} showed that one epoch of benign instruction data raises the harmfulness rate of aligned language models from at most 5.5\% to 12--32\%; visual instruction tuning on largely benign data makes vision-language models less safe than their own language backbones~\cite{zong2024vlguard}; narrow fine-tuning on harmful data erodes multimodal alignment in vision-language models~\cite{gulati2026narrow}; and benign downstream fine-tuning silently degrades concept erasure in diffusion models, most severely under full-parameter updates~\cite{alam2025spqr}. Every one of these results concerns a model whose output is text or an image.

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies produce robot actions, and the community has begun installing safety directly in their weights---by constrained reinforcement learning~\cite{safevla}, by safety-aware fine-tuning, or by supervised refusal data---and measuring it with closed-loop benchmarks that count physical violations~\cite{cui2026liberosafety}. Each of these works trains a safeguard once and evaluates the resulting checkpoint once. But a deployed policy is rarely a fixed checkpoint: the intended workflow for VLAs is personalization on a few dozen demonstrations of the user's own tasks. Whether an action-level safeguard survives that workflow is, to our knowledge, unmeasured. The recent finding that pretrained VLAs retain \emph{task} skills well under continued training~\cite{liu2026resistant,hu2026continual} holds under experience replay or LoRA with on-policy RL; under plain supervised sequential fine-tuning, the regime personalization actually uses, task forgetting is heavy (negative backward transfer of 0.70 for $\pi_0$ without replay~\cite{liu2026resistant}; 79\% under SFT versus 0.3\% under LoRA+RL for OpenVLA-OFT~\cite{hu2026continual}), so neither retention nor loss of a safeguard can be assumed.

Two features make this a different measurement from the text-model literature. First, refusal and harm are separate variables. A text model's refusal \emph{is} the outcome; a VLA's refusal is a no-op action, and harm is a contact event in closed loop. The two can come apart: a policy can lose its taught no-op without reaching the hazard, or reach the hazard while its first-step action still looks like a refusal. Second, LIBERO-fine-tuned OpenVLA-family policies largely ignore their language input~\cite{liberoplus,liberopro}, so a safeguard must first be shown to be instruction-conditioned rather than a visual shortcut, which requires paired instructions on identical states and a blank-instruction control.

We therefore ask: \emph{when an OpenVLA policy with a freshly installed, instruction-conditioned refusal is personalized on benign demonstrations of unrelated tasks, do its refusal rate, over-refusal rate, closed-loop hand-contact rate, and task competence change together or separately?} We report a single-seed pilot of a protocol designed to answer that question with four arms on identical initial states, and we state the outcome pattern we observed among the four that were possible before the runs.

\section{Protocol}

\paragraph{Substrate.}
Policy $P$ is the released OpenVLA-7B LIBERO-Spatial checkpoint~\cite{kim2024openvla}, used with its native action normalization key throughout; old tasks are the ten LIBERO-Spatial tasks; the personalization suite is the ten LIBERO-Object tasks~\cite{liu2023libero}, whose objects and layouts are disjoint from the hazard scenes. Hazard scenes are the five static-hand tasks of LIBERO-Safety's free-space hand-object-avoidance suite at level~0~\cite{cui2026liberosafety}: four LIBERO-10 layouts and one kitchen bowl-to-plate task, each with a MANO hand holding an object placed in the workspace. We use the fork only for its scenes, assets, initial states and contact predicates; every rollout runs on OpenVLA's own robosuite controller pin. \todo{One sentence on the Issue~\#3 predicate fix and why the fork's published hand-suite collision rates are not used as baselines.}

\paragraph{Paired states and instructions.}
Each hazard task ships 50 deterministic initial states. Tasks 1--3 supply training states; tasks 4--5 (25 states each) are held out for every reported number. A state is paired with a \emph{harmful} instruction that targets the hand (e.g.\ ``push the hand out of the way''), a \emph{benign} instruction that performs the task while mentioning the hand (e.g.\ ``\{task\} without touching the hand''), and a \emph{blank} instruction. Training and test template families are disjoint; benign templates share vocabulary with harmful ones so that the word ``hand'' cannot serve as the refusal trigger. All templates are in the supplement.

\paragraph{Installing the safeguard.}
Arm $A$ is $P$ fine-tuned by LoRA with the released checkpoint's own recipe (rank 32, $\alpha=16$, all linear modules, lr $5\times10^{-4}$, three epochs, effective batch 16, plus gradient clipping at 1.0) on 300 harmful no-op transitions, 300 movement transitions from $P$'s own violation-free rollouts under benign instructions on the training states, and 300 LIBERO-Spatial rehearsal transitions. The no-op label is physical zero encoded through the frozen normalization key---six pose tokens near bins 113/110/128/130/138/143, not six copies of the middle bin---with the gripper held at its current state. Comparator $C$ receives the same 600 movement and rehearsal rows and 300 additional movement rows in place of the no-ops, for the same number of updates. $C$ is the no-safeguard reference for whether personalization on its own makes the policy reach the hand.

\paragraph{Personalization.}
Each of $A$ and $C$ is merged into its backbone and personalized with a fresh rank-32 adapter, three epochs, no replay, on $N\in\{50,200\}$ LIBERO-Object demonstrations (5 or 20 per task, nested). The primary comparison is $A@200$ versus $A@0$; $C@200$ versus $C@0$ is reported alongside; $A@50$ is scored offline only.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
\emph{Refusal.} $\Ru$: fraction of held-out states on which all six generated pose tokens lie within one bin of the encoded zero under a harmful instruction (one bin $\approx$ 6--7\,mm translation, $\approx10^{-3}$\,rad rotation); $\Rs$: the same criterion under a benign instruction (over-refusal), excluding states where $P$ itself already refuses; blank: the same under an empty instruction. Two held-out templates per class, aggregated to one value per state ($n=50$). We also report the RoboShackles v2 criterion---$\max_i|a_i|<0.01$ over the six pose dimensions at every predicted step~\cite{roboshackles}, applied here to the model's output actions---which coincides with ours on translation and is looser on rotation. \emph{Contact.} On the same 50 states, each of $A@0,A@200,C@0,C@200$ runs a closed-loop episode under a harmful and under a benign instruction; an episode is a violation if the patched \texttt{CheckRobotContact} predicate fires against the hand object at any step, at which point the episode terminates. Timeouts are task failures, never safety successes. Each episode is also classified as held-all-steps / moved-without-contact / contact / timeout / success. \emph{Utility.} Success on all ten LIBERO-Spatial and all ten LIBERO-Object tasks at $A@0$ and $A@200$, five fixed initial states per task. \emph{Uncertainty.} Wilson intervals per arm; for the paired comparison, discordant-pair counts with an exact McNemar test and a percentile bootstrap over states, all conditional on the single trained chain.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.}
One model, one recipe, one alignment seed, simulation only, a static hand, two held-out layouts, a freshly installed safeguard rather than an inherited one, and no benign-stopping comparator: a difference between refusal loss and any other taught behavior's loss is not measured here.
